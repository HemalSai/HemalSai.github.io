Adversarial Attacks on AI-Powered Cyber Defense Systems and Their Impact on Digital Forensics

An experimental investigation into how adversarial behaviour and monitoring limitations influence AI-assisted intrusion detection and the reliability of evidence available for digital forensic reconstruction.

HDBW University of Applied Sciences · M.Sc. Cybersecurity · 2026

← Research homepage Academic CV ↗
01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Can detection output be trusted as a complete record of an attack?

The research investigated the relationship between adversarial behaviour, AI-assisted detection and digital forensic evidence. A security system may observe an event, but its alerting and downstream correlation layers may represent only part of what happened.

The evaluation focused on four dimensions: evidence completeness, temporal coherence, cross-layer correlation consistency and analytical reliability.

02 / EXPERIMENTAL APPROACH

A segmented, multi-layer security testbed

The research used controlled attack generation, application and authentication telemetry, network monitoring, Python-based detection and centralised security analysis.

Attack Generation→ Target Services→ Network / App / Auth Logs→ Python Detection→ SIEM Correlation→ Forensic Analysis

Tools and methods used across the research included Linux, pfSense, Zeek, Suricata, Wazuh, Python, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, Isolation Forest and TF-IDF-based analysis.

This diagram is a conceptual summary of the research workflow, not a literal network topology or a claim that every component operated simultaneously.

03 / ADVERSARIAL SCENARIOS

Controlled changes to attacker behaviour

Reconnaissance and low-rate scanning

Examined how attack timing, scanning frequency and traffic shaping affect the visibility of reconnaissance activity in monitoring and detection systems.

Authentication anomalies

Investigated suspicious authentication sequences, failed-login bursts and differences in the evidence captured by identity and monitoring layers.

Application-level manipulation

Evaluated application attack patterns including SQL injection, encoded payloads and suspicious requests to assess detection and logging coverage.

Sensor and infrastructure effects

Investigated the influence of network segmentation, sensor placement, routing, NAT and Linux reverse-path filtering on the visibility of security events.

04 / RESEARCH OBSERVATIONS

What the experiments revealed

  • Timing and evasion: Lower-rate behaviour may remain below thresholds used by time-window-based detection.
  • Representation gaps: Encoded or transformed application payloads may evade simple pattern-matching approaches.
  • Layer disagreement: Network, application and authentication monitoring can produce different representations of the same underlying activity.
  • Sensor placement: Incorrect sensor positioning or network configuration can create monitoring gaps even when security tools are operational.

These observations summarize the experimental research. Detailed quantitative results and validated figures should be interpreted alongside the full thesis.

05 / LIMITATIONS & FUTURE RESEARCH

From networked systems to physical computing environments

This research was conducted in a controlled cybersecurity testbed. It does not constitute direct experimental evidence concerning hardware side channels, embedded devices or cyber-physical safety.

Future doctoral research could extend evidence-centred experimental evaluation to hardware, embedded and cyber-physical systems, examining how device state, firmware, physical measurements and network telemetry interact under adversarial conditions.
