RESEARCH CASE STUDY · MASTER'S THESIS · 2026

Adversarial Attacks on AI-Powered Cyber Defense Systems and Their Impact on Digital Forensics

Experimental investigation of how adversarial behaviour and monitoring limitations influence AI-assisted intrusion detection and the evidence available for subsequent forensic reconstruction.

HDBW University of Applied Sciences · M.Sc. Cybersecurity · 2026

← Research homepageAcademic CV ↗

01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Can detection output be trusted as a complete record of an attack?

The research investigated the relationship between adversarial behaviour, AI-assisted detection and digital forensic evidence. A system may observe an event, but its alerting and downstream correlation layers may represent only part of what happened.

Evaluation dimensions: evidence completeness, temporal coherence, cross-layer correlation consistency and analytical reliability.

02 / EXPERIMENTAL APPROACH

A segmented, multi-layer security testbed

The research used controlled attack generation, application and authentication telemetry, network monitoring, Python-based analysis and centralised security monitoring. The following diagram is a conceptual representation of the components described in the academic CV, not a literal topology or proof that all components ran simultaneously.

Attack generation→Target services→Network / app / auth logs→Python detection→SIEM correlation→Forensic analysis

Reported tools and methods: Linux, pfSense, Zeek, Suricata, Wazuh, Python, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, Isolation Forest and TF-IDF-based analysis. Tool usage and experimental configurations will be documented more precisely after the final thesis and code are reviewed.

03 / ADVERSARIAL SCENARIOS

Controlled changes to attacker behaviour

Reconnaissance and low-rate scanning

Explored how traffic rate and timing affect the visibility of reconnaissance activity.

Authentication anomalies

Examined suspicious authentication sequences and the evidence captured across identity and monitoring layers.

Application-level manipulation

Included SQL injection and application attack patterns to assess detection and logging coverage.

Sensor and infrastructure effects

Investigated the influence of sensor placement, network segmentation, routing and reverse-path filtering on observation quality.

04 / OBSERVATIONS

What the experiments suggest

The research identified ways adversarial behaviour and monitoring limitations can create gaps between underlying activity, AI-generated alerts and evidence available for investigation.

  • Timing and evasion: lower-rate behaviour can be less visible to threshold- or time-window-based detection.
  • Representation gaps: encoded or transformed application payloads may not match simple suspicious-pattern checks.
  • Layer disagreement: network, application and authentication observations may differ in coverage and interpretation.
  • Sensor placement: incorrect placement or network configuration can limit visibility even when a monitoring system is running.

05 / LIMITATIONS & NEXT QUESTIONS

From networked systems to physical computing environments

This research was conducted in a controlled cybersecurity testbed. Its findings should not be treated as direct experimental evidence about hardware side channels, embedded devices or cyber-physical safety.

A possible future direction is to apply similarly reproducible, evidence-centred experimental methods to embedded or cyber-physical systems, where physical observations, device state, firmware and network telemetry may disagree. This is a proposed research interest, not a completed experiment.
